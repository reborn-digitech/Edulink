Roles: STUDENT

# The pupil's guide

This guide is for a pupil at a school on Shalaghar, and, in its last part, for a student at a college. You start at `/student`, the "Today" page. The bottom bar on a phone and the rail on a desk carry "Today", "Assignments", "Exams" and "Practice". The "More" menu groups the rest: "Day to day" holds "Timetable", "Messages", "Feedback", "Health card" and "Incidents"; "School life" holds "Certificates", "Social Media", "Student council", "Mentoring", "Sessions", "Past years", "Library" and "Hostel"; "Running things" holds "Me". "Library" and "Hostel" are in the menu only while the school has that module on. At a college the menu also carries "My academics", "My money", "Scholarships", "My account", "My exams", "Placement", "Feedback and grievances" and "What my family sees". The command palette offers "What is due", "Message a teacher", "Share something" and "My exam marks".

## How do I start my day?

Open `/student`. The "Today" page greets you by name with the date and your class. Three tiles show "Today" (your attendance mark, "Not yet" until the teacher marks it, or "No school" on a holiday), "Streak" (school days in a row) and "Attendance" over the last four weeks. "Today’s lessons" lists the day period by period with the teacher and the room, and "Open the timetable" shows the week. "What is due" lists the work you have not handed in, soonest first, with a badge for the due date ("Past due" when it has gone by); "What is coming up" opens `/student/agenda`, and "My fees" opens `/student/fees`. The latest exam marks follow as soon as a teacher saves them. When you first sign in, the page shows your login name; press "Copy" or write it down, then "Hide this message".

## How do I see what is coming up?

Open `/student/agenda` ("What is coming up" on "Today"). It lists your next fortnight in date order: exams, work due, sessions, closures and, where the school allows it, fee dates, each a link to its page. Switch between "Next 14 days" and "Next 30 days". "Today" and "Tomorrow" are named as such.

## How do I see my timetable?

Open `/timetable` ("Timetable"). It shows your real week, class by class, with the teacher of each lesson and "Previous", "This week" and "Next" to move. If a teacher is away, whoever is covering shows in their place. "No timetable yet" means the school has not set its day up; "Nothing timetabled yet" means your classes have no lessons on the grid this week.

## How do I hand in my work?

Open `/student/assignments` ("Assignments", or the quick action "What is due"). Three tabs: "To do", "Handed in" and "Marked". A search box and a "Class" filter narrow a long list.

1. Open the "To do" tab and read the task; files under "From your teacher" open the handouts.
2. Under "Your files", add photographs of your notebook pages if you want; the teacher sees them with your answer.
3. Press "Hand it in", type under "Your answer" ("Type or paste your work here.") and press "Hand it in" again.

The toast says "Handed in 🎉" and your teacher is told. Press "Change it" to replace your words or files until the work is marked; once marked it cannot be changed ("Message your teacher instead"). A late hand-in is shown gently as how many days late, never as a red stamp. The "Marked" tab shows each mark out of the points, a percentage badge and anything the teacher wrote to you. Exam marks are under "Exams", not here.

## How do I see my exam marks and report cards?

Open `/exams` ("Exams", or the quick action "My exam marks"). Three tabs: "Marks", "Upcoming" and "Report cards".

1. "Marks" shows each paper as soon as a teacher saves it; the chips "All exams", "Unit test", "Mid-term" and "Final exam" filter by kind.
2. "Upcoming" shows the published date sheet: day, subject, time and "Out of" for every paper still ahead.
3. "Report cards" lists each card the class teacher has published with its date and overall percentage; open one to read it.

"No marks yet" means nothing has been saved; "No exams coming up" means the school has not announced the next date sheet. Report cards are prepared once the school marks the term finished.

## How do I ask for a certificate?

Open `/student/certificates` ("Certificates"). "Certificates" lists each paper the school has issued to you with "Open" and "Download PDF"; a revoked one stays on the list and says "Revoke".

1. Under "Ask for a certificate", choose the "Certificate" (only the kinds the school has a format for, such as "Bonafide certificate", "Study certificate" or "Character certificate") and say "What it is for" ("A bus pass, a bank account, a scholarship…").
2. Press "Send the request". The toast says "Request sent. The office has been told."

"Your requests" lists each ask as "Waiting for the office", "Issued", "Declined" (with the office's reason) or "Taken back"; press "Take it back" on a waiting one. If the office sets a fee, the line reads "Fee {amount}: pay it on the Fees page, or at the office."; an issued paper shows "Open the certificate". The office issues from your pupil record, so complete the record first (see below).

## What can I see of my fees?

Open `/student/fees` ("My fees" on "Today"). You see fees only if your school shows them to pupils of your age; otherwise the page says "Your school shows fees to pupils from age {age}." or "Your school does not show fees to pupils." When allowed, the tiles show "Outstanding", "Overdue" and "Next due", and "Instalments" lists each with its due date, "Net", "Paid", "Outstanding" and "Status". There is no way to pay here: "Ask a grown-up at home to pay. Fees are paid by your family, never from here."

## How do I practise?

Open `/student/practice` ("Practice"). Chapters are listed for your own class by default; "All classes" and the subject chips widen the list, and "Choose a language" switches the questions. Each card shows the level you are on, "{seen} of {total} tried" and the level badges earned out of 6.

1. Open a chapter and answer. Get two right and it gets harder; get two wrong and it eases off.
2. "Continue where you left off" picks up a half-done chapter; "Your week" counts your questions against your own target (nobody else sees it); "Smart revision" brings back questions you missed, rested for two days, with "Revise now".
3. "Set by your teacher" lists the chapters your teacher set with the target rung and "Reached" or "Keep climbing".

Showing the working on a question ("Show the working for this one?", "Yes, show me") costs that chapter's badge for the run; "I'll keep trying" keeps it. "My journey" (`/student/practice/journey`) shows your weeks in a row, level badges and questions answered, compared only with yourself. There is no leaderboard anywhere.

## How do I message a teacher?

Open `/remarks` ("Messages") and press "New message" to pick a person, or use the quick action "Message a teacher" (`/remarks/new`). A message about you goes to your teacher and is read by your guardians too ("One conversation per pupil, with them and their guardians"). Give a "Subject", write the "Message" and press "Send". Replies arrive in the same thread; a dot marks unread rooms.

## How do I rate my teachers?

Open `/reviews` ("Feedback"). When the school opens a review round, each teacher who teaches you appears as a card with five questions and five stars each. Tap a star for every question, then press "Send". Your name is never shown; teachers and the school see only averages. You can change your answers while the round is open ("Answered — you can change it while the round is open").

## Can I read my health card and incidents?

Open `/student/health` ("Health card") or `/student/incidents` ("Incidents"). Below the age your school sets, both pages say "Ask a grown-up": your family and the school read them for you. From that age you read them yourself, read-only: the blood group, "Allergies", "Conditions", "Medicines", "Diet", "Teachers should know", the "Family doctor" and "Who to call", with the school's "Check-ups"; and each incident the school recorded with "What happened", "What was done" and the "Follow-up" notes. Only the office and your family change the card; only the school writes an incident.

## How do I complete my school record or ask for a change?

Open `/student/record` ("Open the record" on the "Complete the school record" card on "Today", or from "Certificates"). The record is what the school issues your certificates from: your parents' names, place of birth, address, admission number and the fields for the UDISE+ and board filings. A badge reads "Complete" or "{count} missing", and "Still blank: {fields}" names what is empty. "Identity card (PDF)" downloads your card.

1. Press "Ask for a change".
2. Change what is wrong or fill in what is blank; the PEN and the roll number stay the office's.
3. Write "Why this should change" and press "Send to the office".

Nothing changes until the office approves it. "Waiting for the office" shows the ask with each line as "{field}: {from} → {to}"; press "Take it back" to withdraw it. Sending again replaces the request that is waiting. A declined ask shows "The office said: {reason}".

## How do I change how I look, and find my student code?

Open `/student/profile` ("Me"). Under "How you look", press "Change my look" to pick an avatar, set "What should we call you?" and "A bit about you" (do not put your address or phone number there), and press "Save". "Your full name" is what the teacher sees on the attendance list; only the school changes it. "Student code" is what a grown-up at home needs to link to you, with your "Date of birth"; once a guardian is linked, only the office changes the date. The page also shows "Schools you have been to", "Your practice", "Your competitions" and "Your badges" (they arrive on their own and are never taken away). "Download my data" gives everything Shalaghar holds about you as one file.

## How do I post on Social Media?

Open `/bulletin` ("Social Media") and press "Share something" (`/bulletin/new`). Give a "Title", write "What do you want to say?", add "Photos & videos" with a line to "Describe it", and press "Send to my teacher". A teacher reads it before anyone else sees it; "My posts" shows where yours are, as "Waiting for a teacher to check it", "Posted" or "Needs review" with "What your teacher said". Do not include other children's full names. React to posts, comment ("Say something kind…"; a teacher checks your comment first) or "Report" one. On a circular that asks for it, press "Seen and understood". `/bulletin/council` ("Student council") lists the council's members and notices; a member writes a notice there with "Send for review", signed by the council.

## How do I ask for a mentor, book a session or enter a competition?

Open `/mentoring` ("Mentoring"), choose "Who would you like to ask?", say "What would you like help with?" and press "Ask your school". Your school decides first, then a grown-up at home has to say yes; every message can be read by them. Open `/sessions` ("Sessions") to see your free allowance for the year, "Your sessions" and "Open to book": press "Ask for this", say "What do you want from it?" and a guardian must agree before the seat is confirmed; "Join the meeting" appears once it is. A competition your school runs is on your agenda (`/student/agenda`, "Competition"): press "Enrol" on the row, or open it and press "Enter this"; a grown-up at home says yes before you take part (unless they enrolled you themselves), and "Hand in your entry" on the competition's page takes an https link to your work. Where the school switched it on, "This school's board" shows the top places by first name and your own place.

## Where are the library, the hostel and past years?

`/student/library` ("Library"; only when the school has the Library module on) shows "On loan", "Reservations", "Fines" and "The catalogue": search and press "Reserve" to join a queue, or "Leave the queue". `/student/hostel` ("Hostel"; only with the Hostel module) shows your room and bed, "Tonight" (the roll call), "Today's menu" and "Ask for leave": pick the "Kind of leave", "Going to", "Leaving", "Back by" and "Why (optional)", then press "Ask for the leave"; a guardian consents first if you are under eighteen, then the warden decides; "Take the ask back" withdraws it before you go out. `/archive` ("Past years") shows a closed year's attendance, marks, report cards and badges; it never changes again.

## How do I ask Vidura to explain a line of my report card?

On a report card opened from `/exams`, each line has a small link, "Explain this to me". Pressing it opens Vidura's drawer on a new conversation that explains that one line: the mark, out of what, and what the grade or percentage says. Vidura reads the card again from your own pages, so it explains only your own card. The link is shown only where Vidura is switched on for you.

## How do I ask Vidura, and what will it answer?

Vidura is Shalaghar's assistant. Its one way in is the round button at the bottom right of every page, labelled "Ask Vidura" for screen readers; on a phone-width screen it sits above the bottom bar, and above the page's own round quick-action button where one is shown. The button is there only while Shalaghar's operator has Vidura switched on for the platform and for your school; the office does not switch it. As a pupil you also need a plan that includes Vidura; without one the button opens the plan card, with "Subscribe", instead. Your guardian can turn Vidura off for you in their settings. There is no menu entry for Vidura, and opening `/assistant` takes you back to the page you came from with its drawer open.

Pressing the button opens Vidura's drawer. On a computer it docks at the right of the window and the page stays usable beside it, so you can follow the steps while you read them, and it stays open as you move between pages. On a phone it opens as a sheet from the bottom of the screen, and following a link from inside it closes it. Escape closes it, and so does "Close Vidura" (the cross).

It answers two kinds of question: how to use Shalaghar, and the topics in your textbooks, naming the book, the chapter and the page it read under "Sources". It is not a general chatbot: anything else gets one sentence saying what it is for. It reads your own exams, timetable and assignments to answer "when is my next exam". Vidura knows the page it opened beside: "How do I use this page?" or "how do I do this here?" is answered from that page's own help and your own figures shown there. Under "My records", or when Vidura picks it, it answers questions about your own records from the pages you already see (how many assignments are pending, your attendance, your marks in a past class, the latest post on the feed, which messages are unread), counting from the platform's own figures, and under "Sources" it links the post, the circular or the message thread it read; it never tells you anyone else's records.

1. Type in "Your question" and press "Ask". Do not type names, phone numbers or anyone's details. An empty conversation shows "What Vidura is for" and, under "Try asking", three questions for your role and the page you are on (on a page other than your home the first is "How do I use this page?"); pressing one asks it.
2. To send one question a particular way, choose under "Mode for your next question": "Vidura picks" (the default), "Using Shalaghar" (how the platform works, which page and which steps), "My records" (your own figures, counts and links, read from the pages you already see) or "Learning" (topics from the books, with the book, chapter and page under "Sources"). The pick sends only your next question that way, then Vidura picks again, and no pick gets past a refusal. Each answer wears a small label naming the mode it was answered in.
3. If your question matches an assignment of yours, the answer is a "Hint": the idea, the chapter and a guiding question, never the answer. Ask the same question again in the same conversation for the worked answer, unless your teacher has asked for hints only.
4. Answer "Was this helpful?" with "Yes" or "No".
5. "New conversation" starts afresh. "Your conversations" lists the conversations of the last thirty days inside the drawer: press one to re-read it and ask more in it, "Delete this conversation" removes one sooner, and "Back to the conversation" returns.

Answers are set out with headings, numbered steps, bold for what matters, and a small table when your own figures have several rows. A definition is short (about sixty words) and a how-to gives numbered steps (up to about 250 words); an explanation of a topic has four short parts, up to about 350 words in all: the idea, an example, what the book says, and a question to check yourself. If an answer stops at its length limit it says "The answer stopped at its length limit." and "Go on" continues it; "Go on" counts as one of your questions for the day, and an answer can be continued at most twice. Each person has twenty questions a day, a conversation remembers what was said in it only, and each conversation is deleted thirty days after it was last used.

When Vidura cannot answer (its one sentence saying what it is for, a refusal, or an answer saying it is not sure), the answer shows "Vidura could not answer this; a person at your school can." and one button: "Ask your class teacher" (when your class has a class teacher) or else "Ask the school office" (at a college, "Ask the registrar's office"), which opens a message conversation with them. Nothing of your question is copied into the message; you write your own.

What the exam will cover, your teacher can tell you; Vidura explains the topic. A question about hurting yourself is answered with a helpline sentence, and a caring adult at the school (the counsellor, else the head, else the office) is told that you may need someone to talk to, never your words.

## At a college

A college student has the school pages above and these as well. Each says "For colleges" or what the module needs when the college has it off.

### How do I see my lectures, attendance and internal marks?

Open `/student/academics` ("My academics"). "Today's lectures" lists the day by course, room and faculty; "My week" shows the registered courses on the timetable, and a course opens its course file. "My attendance" shows each course against your programme's rule and how many more you may miss. "Internal marks" appear part by part as your HOD releases them, and "My mentor" names your mentor and group. To ask for a shortfall to be condoned, under "Condonations" choose the "Course", the "Ground", attach a "Document", write "What happened" and press "Ask for condonation"; your HOD decides, or the principal. Your request shows "Your request is with your head of department."

### How do I register for the term's courses?

Open `/student/registration` ("Course registration" from "My academics"). Under "Your courses this term", core courses are ticked and fixed; tick the electives, minor or honours courses you want within "{chosen} credits chosen; {min} to {max} allowed", then press "Save my registration". The badge says "Open until {to}" or "Late registration open until {until}"; outside the window it reads "Registration runs {from} to {to}" and nothing can be saved. "No programme yet" means the office has not given you a seat.

### How do I fill my exam form, get my hall ticket and read results?

Open `/student/exams` ("My exams"). "Exam form: {session}" lists the papers with the fee and "Open until {date}"; press "Submit my form" while the window is open, and "Add backlog {course}" to put an open backlog on it. A form is refused without attendance eligibility or with "No-dues not complete: {desks}". Under "Hall ticket and seats", "Hall ticket {number}" downloads the ticket and each paper shows the room and "Seat {seat}". "Where you stand" gives "CGPA {cgpa} · {credits} credits earned"; each published result shows the marks per course and, while the window is open, "Photocopy" and "Revaluation" per course (the fee becomes a demand). "Grade cards and transcripts" downloads each paper issued; "Photocopies and revaluations", "Backlogs" (with the last day to clear each) and "Credit statement" follow.

### How do I use placement?

Open `/student/placement` ("Placement"). "Your record" is read from the exam cell and the office (CGPA, backlogs, attendance); "My resume (PDF)" renders it. Under "Your profile", add "Skills", "Certifications" and "Projects" one per line, tick "Leave me out of this season", "I am going for higher studies" or "I am starting my own venture" if they apply, and press "Save my profile"; the first save is your consent to the cell using your record this season. "Drives" lists each with "Eligible" or why not; tick "I agree to share my profile and record with {company} for this drive." and press "Apply" (a minor's application waits for a guardian's consent), or "Withdraw" until an offer is made. Under "Offers", press "Accept" or "Decline". "Internships": "Propose an internship" with the company, kind, window, dates, hours and supervisor; while it is approved, "Add the day" to the diary and "Upload" the "Report". "Preparation" links aptitude practice; "Alumni mentors" lists free one-to-ones, booked in "Sessions". `/student/placement/talks` answers pre-placement talks with "I'm going" or "Not going"; `/student/placement/training` lets you "Enrol" in soft-skills trainings.

### Where are my money, scholarships, account and feedback?

`/student/money` ("My money") shows this year's ledger by term: "Demand", "Scholarship covers", "Paid" and "You owe". Pay through the college's own gateway or UPI on the fees page, or at its counter; Shalaghar never holds the money. `/student/scholarships` ("Scholarships"; with the Scholarships module) shows "My claims" as the accounts office records them, with "Add" to attach a "Document" ("Scan or photo") to a claim, and "Schemes" with "Open to you". `/student/consent` ("What my family sees") lets an adult student give or take back each grant ("Attendance", "Results", "Fees and money", "Hostel and bus", "Placement", "Messages") with "Let them see it" and "Stop sharing"; a minor's guardians see everything. `/student/account` ("My account") shows those grants with "Change what they see", "Two-step sign-in" with "Manage my sign-in", "Download my data" and your "No-dues" lines as "Cleared" or "Still open". `/student/voice` ("Feedback and grievances"; with the IQAC module) lists "Surveys for you" and "Raise a grievance": choose "What it is about", write "What happened, and what you would like done", optionally attach a document and tick "Keep my name from everyone but the office and the principal", then press "Send to the committee"; the committee must answer within fifteen working days, after which "Take it to the Ombudsperson" is offered.
